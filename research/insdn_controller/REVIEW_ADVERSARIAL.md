# Adversarial review of paper/main.tex (hostile IEEE Trans / Computer Networks reviewer)

Ranked by rejection risk. Checked against results/*.json and literature_check.json (time-boxed pass; not every number re-verified).

1. "Of nine InSDN papers read in full" (abstract, Sec. audit, Discussion, contribution 2)
   - literature_check.json: 12 entries, 10 read=true. Entry 7 (GRU-NTM) is UNSW/BoT-IoT, not InSDN. Entry 0 (Towhid) has no dataset field and uses 9 Scapy features from pcaps, not the CSV. Entry 11 (SICA) is a new dataset with InSDN only as comparison; entry 6 uses InSDN plus CICDDoS. Clearly InSDN-CSV and read: entries 1,4,5,8,9,10 (6), plus 6 and 11 partially. "Nine" is not supported by the JSON; the file's own assessment text says "four InSDN papers read here" (stale).
   - Fix: add a per-paper table (dataset, read, split stated, identifiers) to an appendix and say "N papers that evaluate on InSDN (list), of which M read in full". Recompute N from JSON; update abstract/contribution/discussion consistently. Fix stale "four" in literature_check.json.
2. "none reports a host-grouped split ... three use identifier columns" -> implies guilt
   - Most of these papers do not state their split at all (entry 1: "not stated"); absence of statement is not evidence of random split. "three use identifier columns" rests on entries 4,6,8; entry 6 is partly non-InSDN.
   - Fix: "N papers do not describe their split; K state a random/stratified split; three list identifier columns among inputs (Flow ID/Src IP)". Keep the existing "does not show numbers are wrong" sentence, and move it into the abstract's first mention.
3. Abstract: "Of nine ... their protocols cannot rule out this leakage" + recipe replication "approximating four published feature choices"
   - The recipes are the authors' guess at published choices; a reviewer says this is a straw man. Also "published number is reproducible" for SDNGuardStack is from "our approximation", 5 seeds, default hyperparameters, CI up to +-0.39, i.e. grouped result is uninformative.
   - Fix: say "four generic feature recipes (not reproductions)"; drop "published" from the abstract; for SDNGuardStack report "random split matches reported accuracy; grouped result 0.72-0.78 with CI too wide to rank".
4. Leave-one-host-out (Sec. audit): "detects only 51.6% of the attack flows of the main attacker host"
   - leave_one_host_out.json: holding out 200.175.2.130 removes 110,799 of 343,889 rows (32%) and 6 of 7 attack classes' main training mass (DoS, BFA, Probe etc.); recall differs by class (DoS 0.347, botnet 1.0, U2R 1.0). Only 3 model seeds, no CIs. The 37.9% benign FPR is for one host; the OTHER attacker host 192.168.3.130 has benign FPR 0.833 (in JSON), which the text omits while quoting its 99.7% recall. Selective reporting risk.
   - Fix: add "Removing this host deletes 32% of rows and most DoS/BFA/Probe training mass, so the figure mixes host novelty with class-sample loss; 3 seeds, single numbers, no CI." Report 192.168.3.130 benign FPR 0.83 next to its 99.7% recall. Do not use "only"; say "51.6%".
5. "eight hosts with at least 500 flows (122,307 of 122,868 source IPs occur once)" (abstract + text)
   - Numbers match the JSON. But the inference "dataset has only eight hosts" invites the reply that the 122,307 singletons are spoofed flood sources, not real hosts; this is a property of how InSDN generated floods, so "source-IP grouping" is largely meaningless there. A reviewer may say the 0.907+-0.085 grouped number is mostly where 3 attacker hosts land.
   - Fix: state in the abstract that singletons are spoofed flood sources, that the grouped split therefore decides placement of ~3 attacker hosts, and that the 0.907 gap is a stress test. (Text already has this in the body; hoist one clause to the abstract.)
6. "duplicates do not explain it" / "Redundancy is not the mechanism"
   - dedup_ladder.json: class-covered grouped split gives 0.939 (all rows) but 0.812 (dedup), CI +-0.125 and 1 of 10 splits skipped (class absent in train); the triple split 0.961 vs 0.984. Random split is unaffected by dedup, fine. But "so the drop is not a missing-class artefact" is too strong: grouped runs still change class composition (Sec. recipe admits this) and one split was discarded (selection).
   - Fix: "removing duplicates does not change the random-split score; the held-out-host drop persists (0.939, CI +-0.058, 9 of 10 splits) when all attack classes are present in training, but with wide intervals and a changed class mix." Delete "so the drop is not a missing-class artefact". Also note that the abstract's "(0.939)" omits the skipped split and CI.
7. "A first campaign ... understated the PacketIn effect by more than two orders of magnitude; we report both" and "at least 665x" (abstract)
   - The first campaign's captures were NOT retained (Data availability) so "we report both" is unverifiable beyond the summary file. Also the live study is benign traffic only with 10 runs/mode, one switch; "forwarding application sets what the controller observes" generalises from Ryu simple_switch variants (one controller family, a custom app, no attack traffic).
   - Fix: "(captures not retained; summary file only)" in abstract or footnote; limit claim to "for the Ryu applications we wrote and for stock simple_switch_13"; add Limitations bullet: single controller platform (Ryu) vs ONOS data, no attack traffic in live study.
8. "Using only this controller-observable view ... 0.946 ... 0.89-1.00 on all six withheld families" while "a trivial 'any IP PacketIn' rule scores 0.995" (abstract + Sec. confound)
   - The headline detection result is explicitly undermined by the authors' own confound; the paper still lists "Detection from the controller's view" as contribution 4 and gives MLP vs threshold comparisons. Reviewers will say the detection results carry no information (benign and attack in separate sessions; labels incomplete). Also 44 captures (11 benign) is tiny; CIs over seeds do not capture capture-level variance (the text admits this; capture bootstrap is wider).
   - Fix: reorder abstract so validity threat comes first; rename claim "feasibility, confounded by session identity; not evidence of attack-detection ability". Report the capture-level bootstrap CI in the abstract rather than the seed-only value.
9. Novelty wording: "We found no work that measures, inside InSDN, what the controller observed"; "what is new is the paired, quantified gap on an SDN dataset"
   - literature_check.json caution: Lanvin 2022 and Liu 2022 "should be read before finalising any 'first' claim", and "several 2021-2026 InSDN papers remain unread". Salman et al. already report InSDN 99% -> ~82% drop (attributed differently). A search is described as "October 2026" evidence only (good), but the contributions list still says "what is new".
   - Fix: "to our knowledge, and within the papers we could access (Lanvin, Liu and others not read in full)"; cite Salman et al. next to the 0.907 gap and state how our attribution differs.
10. Contradictions / stale remnants
   - Abstract says "Second, we audit ..." but no "First" label precedes (structure leftover). The intro lists "seven contributions; first two main" but the abstract's headline order is different.
   - Result JSON keys E_test_rows_duplicating_train_row_splitA / E_dup_splitB/C and the experiment list item "(E) label-shuffle sanity check" in the detection section reuse letter E for a different thing than the audit's "E" duplicate measure; a reader of Table tab:audit and JSON can confuse them. Rename audit key to dup_* or the detection item to (F).
   - Sec. confound/limitations mention "two datasets is the evidence base" yet the conclusion/abstract speak of "SDN intrusion-detection research" in general. Replace with "on InSDN (and CICIDS2017 as a non-SDN comparison)".
11. Statistics: "10 seeds" vs "5 seeds" vs "3 seeds" mixed; CIs "up to +-0.39/+-0.241"
   - Seed CIs only reflect resampling of the same dataset, with t-intervals on n=10 of highly skewed per-seed values (e.g. grouped per_seed ranges 0.66-1.00). Mean +- 95% t-CI of a bimodal distribution is misleading; the 0.907+-0.085 abstract figure hides that. Also repeated groups across seeds are not independent.
   - Fix: report median and min-max (already "worst 0.66") for grouped splits; state n per number; use bootstrap over hosts rather than t-interval.
12. Missing limitations
   - No attack traffic in the live study; offline replay only; ONOS captures only from InSDN's single testbed; window labels derived from the same CSV the paper criticises (circularity: labelling windows by 5-tuple matching to flows whose label coverage is partial); parser loss lower-bound; CICIDS2017 time-split disagreement unexplained (acknowledged); single classifier family (XGBoost) in the leakage audit; no adversarial/evasion analysis (stated out of scope). Add one consolidated Limitations paragraph listing these.
