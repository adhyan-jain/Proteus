\section{Forwarding-Application Case Study: Mechanism and Sensitivity}\label{sec:live}
InSDN reflects a single forwarding configuration. To evaluate whether controller-visible telemetry is fundamentally dictated by forwarding application design, we constructed a controlled testbed (Mininet, Open vSwitch, six hosts; a Ryu OpenFlow~1.3 controller with configurable flow-match granularity) and recorded its control channel using our OpenFlow parser. 

Three operational modes were evaluated for each learned destination: (i) MAC-pair forwarding; (ii) per-flow 5-tuple forwarding for TCP/UDP with a 10\,s idle timeout; and (iii) per-flow 5-tuple forwarding with a 2\,s idle timeout. In the 5-tuple modes, other IP traffic receives an \texttt{(in\_port, eth\_src, eth\_dst, proto)} rule and ARP receives an ARP-specific entry, preventing coarse entries from overshadowing granular flows. The controller polls flow statistics at 1\,s intervals. To isolate forwarding mechanics, traffic is purely benign: five client hosts repeatedly issue HTTP requests (fetching files of 16\,KB to 1\,MB, 80\% of requests) or ICMP pings (20\%) to an HTTP server with randomized Poisson pacing over 90\,s (total capture $\sim$99\,s). Each mode was executed across 10 independent runs (seeds 0--9), completing 26\,891 requests. Results report mean and 95\% confidence intervals over runs (Table~\ref{tab:live}). We repeated the study on a loop-free three-switch topology (Table~\ref{tab:live_l3}).

\textbf{Granularity Dictates PacketIn Volume.} MAC-pair forwarding produced only $12.3\pm2.3$ TCP PacketIns per run. In contrast, per-flow forwarding generated $10\,422\pm202$ (10\,s idle) and $10\,392\pm249$ (2\,s idle) PacketIns per run: an increase of 665$\times$ to 1\,752$\times$ (10\,s idle) and 668$\times$ to 1\,780$\times$ (2\,s idle) across all ten paired runs (larger in 10 of 10 runs; two-sided sign-test $p=0.002$). 

This represents approximately 11--12 TCP PacketIns per completed connection. This elevated rate arises from software datapath buffering and the absence of OpenFlow barrier synchronization: in reactive learning controllers that forward full frames without switch-side buffer IDs, back-to-back packets of an initial TCP exchange (e.g., SYN, SYN-ACK, initial ACK) trigger concurrent table misses before the switch kernel flow table is updated by the controller's asynchronous FlowMod. FlowMods (19.1 vs.\ about 10\,440 and 10\,560 per run) track PacketIns directly. To ensure statistical stability, the initial five runs (seeds 0--4) were extended to ten pre-planned runs (seeds 0--9); trends and ratios hold identically across both subsets.

\textbf{Idle Timeout Governs Flow Table Visibility.} Per-flow entries returned per statistics reply dropped from $153.1\pm2.4$ to $36.9\pm0.5$ when the idle timeout was reduced from 10\,s to 2\,s (a ratio of 0.234 to 0.246 across all ten pairs). Because inactive entries expire between 1\,s polling cycles, a shorter timeout purges roughly 75\% of entries before they are polled. In MAC-pair mode, replies contain solely the table-miss rule and MAC-pair entries ($9.5\pm0.03$ per reply); zero entries match IP or transport fields, replicating the pattern observed in InSDN.

\textbf{Shadowing Effects in Coarse Forwarding.} In an earlier flawed application campaign, MAC-pair entries installed for initial ARP/ping traffic matched subsequent TCP connections between the same hosts. Consequently, TCP connections bypassed the controller, producing only $43.4\pm30.1$ TCP PacketIns per run (4$\times$ MAC-pair). This demonstrates how rule shadowing in coarse forwarding policies can inadvertently blind the controller to higher-layer traffic.

\textbf{Run Reliability and Edge Anomalies.} In one run (5-tuple, 2\,s idle, seed 4), 41 client requests failed due to transient socket timeouts during startup, distributed across all five clients; the remaining 29 one-switch runs and all 30 three-switch runs completed with zero failures. This run is retained in all reported metrics.

\textbf{Multi-Switch Topology Verification.} On a three-switch linear topology (Table~\ref{tab:live_l3}), the contrast persists: $18.0\pm5.0$ TCP PacketIns under MAC-pair versus $10\,881\pm794$ and $10\,676\pm804$ under per-flow forwarding (391$\times$ to 1\,509$\times$ higher across all paired runs). Flow entries returned to a 1\,s poller fall from $112.4\pm2.3$ to $27.0\pm0.4$ under the 2\,s timeout.

\textbf{Verification on Stock Ryu Switch.} To verify that MAC-pair behavior is not an artifact of our custom application, we evaluated Ryu's standard, unmodified \texttt{simple\_switch\_13} across 5 independent 60\,s runs (2\,982 requests, zero failures). Of 91 FlowMods installed, 86 match \texttt{(in\_port, eth\_dst, eth\_src)} and 5 are table-miss rules; none contains IP or transport fields. The controller received an average of 73.6 PacketIns per run (range 69--76).

\textbf{Scope and Realism.} This testbed serves as an empirical mechanism demonstration of forwarding dynamics in software Open vSwitch environments. It measures control-plane observability rather than attack detection accuracy. It does not emulate hardware TCAM constraints, proactive flow installation, or production carrier-grade SDN fabrics.
